# TALI-W Morphing Wing Architecture

## Objective

Produce the largest useful aerodynamic control envelope for the lowest total deployed-system mass.

The wing is not a decorative mechanical arm. It is a variable-geometry lifting and control surface whose structure, deployment, inertia, and aerodynamic state are part of the flight-control problem.

## Candidate morphing variables

The research programme should evaluate:

- span extension/retraction;
- sweep change;
- local twist;
- camber change;
- segmented trailing-edge surfaces;
- leading-edge high-lift devices;
- alula-like vortex/control devices;
- deployable drag panels or spoilers;
- fan-tail area and angle;
- differential left/right geometry.

Not every variable should be implemented. The preferred architecture is the smallest set that provides adequate efficiency, stall margin, control authority, and terminal braking.

## Structural concept

Candidate construction:

- high-specific-stiffness primary spars;
- lightweight ribs or inflatable/tensioned secondary structure;
- tensioned textile or thin composite aerodynamic skin;
- locking joints that carry steady aerodynamic load without continuous actuator torque;
- heavy actuation as close to the torso/root as practical;
- low distal mass to reduce rotational inertia and root loads.

Materials are candidates, not requirements, until analysed and tested. Carbon-fibre composites, aluminium/titanium fittings, high-strength pins, bearings, reinforced fabrics, films, and inflatable structures are all trade-study options.

## High-lift landing configuration

Landing mode is expected to use a substantially different geometry from fast flight. Candidate changes include:

- increased projected area;
- reduced sweep;
- increased camber;
- leading-edge devices;
- tail expansion;
- controlled high angle of attack;
- distributed drag increase.

The objective is to remove energy aerodynamically before high-power terminal assist is required.

## Alula-inspired devices

The biological alula is relevant as inspiration for high-angle-of-attack flow control. TALI-W should investigate whether a small deployable leading-edge device can delay local separation or improve control authority in flare/slow-flight conditions.

This must be validated for the actual Reynolds number and wing geometry. Biological function does not guarantee that a geometrically similar device will work on a human-scale engineered wing.

## Fan-tail concept

A deployable tail or equivalent aft aerodynamic surface may provide:

- pitch trim and stability;
- yaw authority;
- additional drag;
- increased low-speed lifting area;
- flare control;
- redundancy with wing differential control.

Its value should be compared against mass, structural moment, stowage volume, and interference with the body/wing wake.

## Deployment safety

The wing shall not transition directly from stowed to flight authority without verifying:

- mechanical lock state;
- left/right configuration agreement;
- actuator health;
- load state;
- clearance;
- sensor plausibility.

Asymmetric or incomplete deployment shall have a defined control response and recovery state.

## Verification priorities

Before full-scale occupied research:

1. coupon/material tests;
2. joint and lock-cycle tests;
3. static structural test;
4. deployment dynamics on a fixed rig;
5. wind-tunnel or equivalent aerodynamic characterization;
6. sub-scale unmanned flight;
7. full-scale unmanned load and control validation.
